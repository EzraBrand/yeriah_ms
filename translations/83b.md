## Notes
[^1]: The opening repeats “that the Torah” from 83a:42. Micah 7:20 supplies “kindness to Abraham”; “Torah” here need not mean only the Pentateuch. Bava Batra 25b connects facing north with wealth and the northern placement of the table, continuing 83a:40–42. The prince of Esau is an opposing supernal power in the commentary; the curtain is retained as its spatial image. [Primary text: Bava Batra 25b](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.25b). {source: Interfolio continuity and primary references.}
[^2]: Psalm 89:3, עולם חסד יבנה. The translation preserves the commentator’s reading of עולם as “world” and חסד as both kindness and the attribute Chesed. His objection concerns how a side without desire can produce the lower hypostasis through natural heat. The extra wording שאם שחס׳[?] remains syntactically uncertain. {source: Biblical wording and unresolved syntax.}
[^3]: The source reads אינו, “is not,” after “does not deny”; the resulting double negative is retained, although the surrounding defense appears to require an affirmation that Gedulah is mercy. No word is deleted or emended. The abbreviation מה׳ after Gevurah is not expanded with certainty. The main text consequently remains difficult at this point. {source: Image-checked negative and abbreviation.}
[^4]: Genesis 22:1. The fire/water question belongs to the commentator’s argument about Abraham and Isaac; its precise inferential force is compressed. The translation does not reverse it or supply an unstated conclusion. Genesis 15:1 supplies “Do not fear, Abram”; “Shield of Abraham” is the conclusion of the Amidah’s opening blessing. {source: Biblical and liturgical references.}
[^5]: The added letter he distinguishes אברהם from אברם (Genesis 17:5); the commentary identifies it with Binah above Abraham. ובזה׳ is read as a reference to the Zohar. An exact location for the following Aramaic dictum has not been established; its attribution is the manuscript’s, not an independently verified citation. {source: Name interpretation and unlocated citation.}
