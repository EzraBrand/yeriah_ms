## Apparatus | Cycles, figures, and witness variants
| Passage | Function in the commentary | Witness evidence or identification |
|---|---|---|
| Binding and ark (5–21) | Two receptacles image the upper world; Abraham, Isaac, and the ram compress the seven lower *sefirot* into the two arms and the mediating line. | Oxford confirms the principal lemmata and clarifies “one from a city and two from a family”; JTS alone is translated in the line grid. |
| Forty days (22–27) | Flood and Sinai each suspend ordinary embodied existence, leaving Noah or Moses as the human intellect. | Oxford reads ומשה עמד securely; the corresponding JTS words remain marked uncertain. |
| Waters and knowledge (28–30) | JTS links Flood waters, knowledge as union, the shared crown, and *Binah*. | JTS דיעה (“knowledge”) contrasts with Oxford רעה (“evil”), producing a consequential interpretive difference. |
| Six and seven (31–37) | The six extremities act through a complete cycle; the seventh performs release and receives renewed souls from *Binah*. | Oxford preserves ושתא אלפי הוי עלמא, whereas JTS is corrupt or heavily abbreviated. |
| This world and primordial generations (38–42) | Noah’s generation, the days of the Messiah, Adam’s demons, and the tenth generation are coordinated across cycles. | Oxford preserves a fuller, substantially different sequence; it is comparative evidence, not a silent correction of JTS. |

## Notes
[^1]: The opening completes the sentence begun at 79b:45. “The wonders of Him who is perfect in knowledge” echoes Job 37:16. The manuscript’s `בחלק[?] המין הישראלי` is retained provisionally as “the portion of the Israelite people.” {source: Biblical and textual note.}
[^2]: Lines 2–4 appear to quote the *Yeri'ah*. The *alef* at the head of the book is interpreted by notarikon as *Keter*, above *Binah*. The wording of line 3 is insecure: the final word may be סוד, “secret,” rather than the transcribed סוף, “end.” {source: Textual and sefirotic note.}
[^3]: The comparison treats the Binding and the ark as receptacles and as images of the upper world. The seven included in three are the seven lower *sefirot* compressed into Abraham and Isaac, the two arms (*Chesed* and *Pachad* or *Gevurah*), and the ram as the mediating line (*Tiferet*). The waters issuing from Abraham annul the desire disclosed through Isaac. The readings at lines 10 and 13–15 remain too uncertain to normalize. “And Ham is Esau” preserves the manuscript’s feminine copula (היא for הוא) and pairs two wicked figures, one in the ark and one present potentially at the Binding. {source: Sefirotic, interpretive, and textual note.}
[^4]: “Noah found favor in the eyes of the Lord” is Genesis 6:8; the divine name is interpreted as the upper pool. Isaac’s sowing, blessing, and increasing greatness draw on Genesis 26:12–13. The Noah phrase may reflect Genesis 7:17–18 (“the waters increased/prevailed”), but the manuscript appears to read ויגדל and remains uncertain. The final quoted clause in line 20 is not securely resolved; it probably gestures toward the names of Esau’s chiefs “according to their families” in Genesis 36:40. The commentary uses these correspondences to argue that human types recur from cycle to cycle. {source: Biblical, cyclical, and textual note.}
[^5]: The first half of the lemma adapts Genesis 7:17; the second compares it with Moses’ forty days on Sinai in Exodus 24:18 and 34:28. Forty is read as the four legs of the Chariot multiplied by ten. The Flood annuls ordinary human existence, while Moses’ ascent annuls his bodily powers and leaves the human intellect. Noah is likewise the surviving intellect, accompanied in the ark by the powers nearest to it. The subject before “stood on Mount Sinai” and several phrases in lines 23–27 remain uncertain. {source: Biblical, philosophical, and textual note.}
[^6]: The lemma combines the Flood with Isaiah 11:9: “for the earth shall be full of knowledge ... as the waters cover the sea.” “Adam knew” evokes Genesis 4:1 or 4:25 and gives דעה the sense of union. “Two kings cannot use one crown” is the lunar tradition in b. Hullin 60b; the present passage then identifies the relevant grade as *Binah*. Whether ים, “sea,” names *Binah*, *Malkhut*, or another symbolic locus here is unresolved. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, and sefirotic note.}
[^7]: Genesis 7:11 begins, “In the six-hundredth year of Noah’s life.” The commentary coordinates the six hundred years, the six thousand years of a cosmic cycle, and the six extremities; every seventh millennium then receives new souls from *Binah*. Oxford’s ושתא אלפי הוי עלמא clarifies the allusion to the maxim that the world endures six thousand years (b. Rosh Hashanah 31a; b. Sanhedrin 97a), but most of the JTS continuation in line 32 and the first word of line 33 remain too insecure to normalize. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, cosmological, and textual note.}
[^8]: The six extremities are the six lower *sefirot* from *Chesed* through *Yesod*; their completed action is followed by the seventh, *Malkhut*, figured as sabbatical release. “Ten generations from Adam to Noah” is m. Avot 5:2. The upper pool is *Binah*; the uncertain fifties probably evoke *Binah*’s fifty gates and the Jubilee declaration “it shall be holy to you” in Leviticus 25:12. The lemma “For at first the Messiah came” is secure in Oxford, but the relation between the primordial Adamic cycle and the future messianic cycle remains the commentary’s own difficult cyclical claim. {source: Mishnaic, biblical, and sefirotic note.}
[^9]: The explanation in lines 38–39 quotes the rabbinic dictum that the only difference between this world and the days of the Messiah is subjection to kingdoms (b. Berakhot 34b; b. Sanhedrin 99a). “Daughters were born to them” and “the sons of God saw the daughters of humanity” are Genesis 6:1–2. Adam’s begetting of demons and *lilin* during 130 years under ban is b. Eruvin 18b; JTS appears to read 130, while Oxford reads forty. Oxford’s continuation identifies the tenth generation with Noah and later reaches Abraham and *Malkhut*, but JTS line 42 instead breaks off mid-sentence and continues on folio 80b. The obscure wording in lines 40–42 is therefore left visibly uncertain. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, inter-folio, and comparative-witness note.}
