## Notes
[^1]: The opening completes the sentence at 81a:46 about the nations’ princes receiving abundance. Exodus 7:18 supplies the dead fish and the river’s stench. “Circumcise … again” recalls Joshua 5:2, while the first day of the month belongs to the base text’s compressed chronological scheme. “Crown” translates עטרה in the bodily image; the subsequent mystical referent is *Shekhinah*, provisionally expanding ש׳. The clause about ordinary practice does not instruct total withdrawal from embodied life. Joined rows 2–4, 8–9, and 14–16 are printed once, with explicit fragment notices; their original crop order is documented in the Hebrew source. {source: Biblical, lexical, and layout note.}
[^2]: Exodus 4:25 supplies Zipporah and the flint; Genesis 15:10 supplies the bird not being divided. The commentary links צפרה, “Zipporah,” צפור, “bird,” and צפירת תפארה, “diadem of beauty” (Isaiah 28:5). צור, “flint,” is also linked to צר/צורר, “adversary/oppressor.” The English changes rendering only where the Hebrew form changes; the wordplay is therefore given explicitly here. The phrase about returning home in line 12 remains broken and is not reconstructed from a standard biblical text. {source: Biblical wordplay and textual note.}
[^3]: The forty days recall Genesis 8:6. The exposition relates forty to the four legs of the Chariot; four times ten equals forty, but the factor ten is explanatory and is not written in this line. The short interlinear insertion in line 13 remains uncertain in both reading and syntax. “Light of the world” was checked in Main_14_F; the former “hidden light” is not retained merely because it suited the surrounding subject. {source: Numerical, biblical, and image-review note.}

[^4]: The wording recalls Moses’ abstention in Exodus 34:28 and Deuteronomy 9:9; “bread of adversity” recalls Isaiah 30:20. The crop sequence puts line 18 between two parts of a sentence: the sentence runs 17 → 19 → 20. The English labels that continuation rather than moving the numbered row. The roots in ירחיק, “distances,” and הדביק, “attached,” are retained as different verbs. Genesis 8:5 supplies the diminishing waters, tenth month, and mountain tops; the seventh millennium is the commentator’s interpretation, not the wording of Genesis. {source: Biblical and syntactic note.}
[^5]: The clause about darkness is uncertain and appears difficult beside “light of the world.” The transcription is not changed to make the two agree. The abbreviation מ׳ה׳ at line 24 is unresolved. At lines 27–29, the sequence is provisionally understood as denying independent rule while granting derivative power; the referents and expansions of מה׳ remain interpretive. {source: Textual and interpretive note.}
[^6]: Genesis 8:6–7 recounts opening the window and sending the raven. Both Great Parchment witnesses negate sending, though they differ in person and tense. “Cutting the shoots” is the image for a destructive separation of divine powers; compare Aher’s action in Hagigah 14b–15a. It is retained as a technical image rather than replaced by a general word such as “heresy.” {source: Biblical, rabbinic, and witness note.}
[^7]: Psalm 147:9 supplies “to the young ravens that call.” The commentary distinguishes Ishmael’s prince from Esau’s: both belong to judgment, but Ishmael’s receives upper waters identified as *Chesed*. The singular pronouns after plural “young ravens” follow the Hebrew’s shift to the prince. “Going out and returning” and the drying waters adapt Genesis 8:7. {source: Biblical and interpretive note.}
