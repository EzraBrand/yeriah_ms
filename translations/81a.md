## Notes
[^1]: The opening completes 80b:54, “they apprehended only … in the bride.” Genesis 7:20 uses singular אמה after fifteen. The commentary contrasts this with plural אמות and associates the divine name with *Atarah*; the unfinished wording in line 2 prevents a complete reconstruction. יה = 10 + 5 = 15. Oxford’s explicit כתר עליון is a parallel, not a replacement for the JTS explanation. The abbreviation ע׳ is expanded as *Atarah* provisionally, following the local transcription notes. {source: Biblical, numerical, and comparative note.}
[^2]: The exposition combines the Flood’s ויגוע (Genesis 7:21) with the patriarchal death formula ויגוע ויאסף (Genesis 25:8). Genesis 7:22 supplies the breath of life and dry ground; Job 32:8 supplies “the breath of the Almighty gives them understanding.” The commentary distinguishes a superior death, in which souls return to *Binah*, from the extinction of animal spirits derived outside the Chariot. The word חרבה, “dry ground,” is linked to חורבן, “destruction.” Line 7’s “wing” remains uncertain. {source: Biblical and lexical note.}
[^3]: Genesis 7:23 supplies Noah and the ark; Isaiah 3:11 supplies recompense. The image reads “with his sons … six extremities,” replacing the earlier “with a belly … two extremities.” The text calls the Righteous One “seventh”; it is not silently renumbered to fit another sefirotic order. Image review also gives “at the head of the corner,” echoing Psalm 118:22. Two kings sharing one crown recalls b. Hullin 60b. The interlinear “seventh” takes precedence over the struck word, but the uncertain reading of that deletion is still shown. “Will delight” is provisional. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, and textual note.}
[^4]: Genesis 8:1 supplies God’s remembrance of Noah and the creatures. Psalm 115:12 has “the Lord has remembered us,” but the abbreviation transcribed יה׳ remains uncertain and is not replaced with the biblical spelling. The reading דבק, “cleaving,” is supported by the line image and Oxford. Lines 17–23 connect divine unity with the distribution of abundance among creatures. The isolated ד׳ in line 19 and `היכל[?]` in line 21 are not silently repaired. Line 22 reads הלשון, “the tongue,” in both the image and Oxford; the exposition identifies it with birds. This difficult identification is retained. The interlinear יתרון, “advantage,” may belong beside the lemma, as in Oxford, but remains at its manuscript line position. Wild and domesticated animals are distinguished by the relative coarseness of the four elements. {source: Biblical and image-review note.}
[^5]: The valley of Jehoshaphat recalls Joel 4:2, 12 (English numbering 3:2, 12); “the king’s valley” is Genesis 14:17. Recompense according to deeds recalls Jeremiah 17:10, and the recompense of hands is from Isaiah 3:11. The change from singular “his hands” to plural “to them” is in JTS and is retained; Oxford instead has “to him.” *Atarah* and “the Name” expand uncertain abbreviations. {source: Biblical, comparative, and textual note.}
[^6]: Genesis 8:3–5 supplies diminishing waters and the tenth month. The adjective “bitter” is part of the mystical recasting; compare the bitter waters of Numbers 5:18. Leviticus 27:32 supplies the tenth being holy. Image review replaces the unintelligible lemma `ען יעשהו[?]` with `עד עשירי`, “until the tenth”; Oxford has עד העשירי. The “forgiveness” phrase in line 30 has no counterpart in this Oxford passage and remains syntactically incomplete. No numerical equation is asserted here: “tenth” is an ordinal assigned to Thought. {source: Biblical, image-review, and comparative note.}
[^7]: Exodus 3:5 supplies removing the shoes, but JTS and the English retain the singular. Exodus 3:2 says that the bush was not consumed; the base text recasts it as “the bush shall not burn.” The exposition locates change in the lower recipients rather than above, while still describing a change toward mercy in the following lines. This tension is retained. The staff and hand refer to Exodus 4:4; JTS has second-person “your hand,” not the biblical “his hand.” {source: Biblical and interpretive note.}
[^8]: Exodus 4:6–7 describes Moses’ hand becoming leprous and returning to flesh; Numbers 12:10 describes Miriam as leprous as snow. The commentary joins the two scenes and provisionally identifies Miriam with the moon. “Redness” in line 39 is a conjectural expansion of אד׳; “flesh” is not silently translated as mercy. The accusation/defense vocabulary in line 36 is juridical, but no specific rabbinic quotation is claimed. {source: Biblical and textual note.}
[^9]: Exodus 4:9 and 7:17–21 describe water becoming blood. Here the negative “you shall not turn” is shared by JTS and Oxford; it must not be normalized to the biblical positive event. JTS uses תשוב rather than a regular causative form; the transitive English follows the exposition’s address to Moses, with that grammatical difficulty left explicit here. The repeated מימימי in line 41 remains uncertain. “A consuming fire” echoes Deuteronomy 4:24. The princes are the heavenly counterparts of nations; the claim that a nation falls only after its prince does is preserved without assigning an unverified exact rabbinic source. The sentence about receiving abundance continues at 81b:1. {source: Biblical, comparative, and syntactic note.}
