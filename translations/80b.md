## Apparatus | Descent, repentance, and ascent
| Passage | Function in the commentary | Source or identification (provisional) |
|---|---|---|
| Sons of God and daughters of humanity (1–16) | Human intellect descends toward material powers; Adam’s demons and the sequence of generations provide the model. | Genesis 6:1–2; b. Eruvin 18b; Oxford preserves a clearer but materially different sequence. |
| Abraham and Sodom (17–23) | Abraham as *Chesed* seeks mercy, while divine mercy and judgment are separated within the biblical wording. | Genesis 18:23; Genesis 6:3–4; Psalm 144:15; “thus,” “inasmuch,” and Moses each have the numerical value 345. |
| Penitents and the middle line (25–31) | The human intellect’s fall into the earthly is reversed through repentance; matter and form are raised together. | *Tiferet* as the middle line; b. Berakhot 34b. |
| Second month and ark (32–40) | The second month, the ark, the fifty gates, and the windows of heaven are coordinated with *Binah*. | Genesis 7:11; b. Hagigah 13a; several words in the lemma and its exposition remain unresolved. |
| Fifteen cubits and straight line (41–55) | Flood waters descend from Thought; the six extremities and the bride rise, while Noah and his sons become channels. | Genesis 7:19–20; *Yah* = 15; the bride = *Malkhut*; Thought is provisionally *Keter*. |

## Notes
[^1]: Lines 1–3 continue the unfinished sentence at 80a:42. “The sons of God saw the daughters of humanity” is Genesis 6:2. The marginal material in lines 4, 6, 8, and 10 preserves the tradition that during the 130 years in which Adam was under ban he begot demons, spirits, and *lilin* (b. Eruvin 18b). The commentary reads that tradition as an impairment in *Malkhut*, within the Chariot. The first words of line 1 and the exact relation between main text and marginal glosses remain uncertain. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, codicological, and textual note.}
[^2]: The lemma and exposition in lines 9–12 are textually insecure and discuss the generations and cyclical return. Oxford reads “the tenth generation returned to Noah, and from Peleg to Abraham,” which probably clarifies JTS line 12 and evokes the ten generations from Noah to Abraham in m. Avot 5:2. A recheck of the image (27 September 2026) confirms both lemmata as Oxford reads them: line 9 `ודור עשירי ישובו לנח` (earlier draft `נהר עשירי יכלבו לבד`) and line 12 `ומפלגא לאברהם` (earlier `ואפלה`). The same recheck replaced the earlier reading `לא קבלתי` (“I received no tradition”) with `נתנו לנו קבלה` (“they gave us a tradition”), so the commentator does not disclaim a tradition here. The exposition’s identification of the tenth generation with Adam (`הוא אדם[?]`) is unexpected, since Noah is the tenth generation from Adam, and the final word should be checked again. Genesis 11:4 then supplies the city and tower in lines 13–14. {source: Mishnaic, comparative-witness, and textual note.}
[^3]: In line 23 JTS reads `והולידו את בני האלהים מהבנות`: the particle `את` makes “the sons of God” the object (“they begot the sons of God from the daughters”), whereas Oxford (`והולידו בני האלהים מהבנות`) and Genesis 6:4 make them the subject. The translation keeps the JTS syntax. Abraham as the man of *Chesed* intercedes for Sodom in Genesis 18:23. “My spirit shall not abide” and the men of renown are from Genesis 6:3–4. “Happy is the people for whom it is thus” is Psalm 144:15; שככה, בשגם, and משה each have the numerical value 345, an equivalence invoked in b. Hullin 139b. “They compare the form to its Maker” echoes Genesis Rabbah 27:1. The JTS lemma in lines 22–23 departs from the wording of Genesis 6:4 and remains visibly uncertain. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, gematric, and textual note.}
[^4]: Genesis 7:11 links the second month with the opening of the windows of heaven. The fifty gates belong to *Binah*; “Do not inquire into what is beyond you” is cited in b. Hagigah 13a. The commentary parses “the seventeenth” (`בשבעה עשר`) into its parts, “seven” (37) and “ten” (38); the translation keeps the two parts apart. Several abbreviations in lines 32–40 are unresolved. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, and textual note.}
[^5]: The lemma in line 41 reads `ויכסו ההרים ט' כלה מישור`. Oxford has `כי כלה מישור` (“for it is all level”), and the gloss at line 45 (`כי כולה מישור`) supports that sense; the gloss at line 47 (`ר"ל הכלה שהיא מלכות`) rereads the word as `הכלה`, “the bride.” Both senses are given. Lines 41–44 interpret Genesis 7:19–20: the fifteen cubits correspond to the divine name *Yah* (יה = 15) and to Thought, while the mountains are the six extremities. The isolated sign in line 41 has not been expanded. {source: Biblical, rabbinic, numerical, and textual note.}
[^6]: The outer-margin glosses at lines 45, 47, 49, and 53 identify the bride with *Malkhut* and describe her ascent along the straight line; they have been translated at their physical line positions rather than silently inserted into the main text. Noah and his sons are channels extending from Thought toward the lower world. “No eye has seen” is Isaiah 64:3, and “under all the heavens” returns to Genesis 7:19. The sentence continues on folio 81a; the catchword in line 55 is illegible. {source: Codicological, biblical, and sefirotic note.}
