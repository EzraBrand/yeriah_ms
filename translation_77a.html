Notes

  1. The opening completes the exposition of Ps. 15:2 begun on fol. 76b. The movement “from below upward” appears to explain the ordered verbs: first walking in integrity (Binah/Mercy), then acting in righteousness (Chokhmah/Judgment). The syntax of line 5 is difficult, but the point seems to be that the pairing is deliberate. Translator’s interpretation.↩
  2. The lemma combines Gen. 2:13 and Gen. 10:8; “upon your belly shall you go” is Gen. 3:14, while “hunt game for me” is Gen. 27:3. The wordplay links גיחון with גחון, belly. Biblical identifications.↩
  3. The passage invokes “Rabad,” probably the commentary on Sefer Yetzirah transmitted under that attribution. The right/left assignments as currently transcribed are internally difficult: Gedulah and Chesed are normally synonyms, yet line 21 appears to place them on opposite sides. This needs manuscript collation rather than harmonization. Text-critical caution.↩
  4. The lemma joins Gen. 2:14, fertility language based on Gen. 1:28, and “These are the generations of Esau” (Gen. 36:1). Its association of proliferation with material appetite converts the river’s name into a moral-psychological symbol. Source analysis.↩
  5. The Jacob narrative is Gen. 32:25–29. The identification of Esau’s angel with Samael is widespread in rabbinic and medieval sources; compare Genesis Rabbah 77–78. The uncertain “Gabriel” in line 34 conflicts with the surrounding identifications and should be rechecked. Source and textual note.↩
  6. “Weak he” (ה״א רפה) refers to the final letter of the Tetragrammaton, conventionally mapped to Malkhut/Shekhinah. “Daughter of Jacob” similarly presents the feminine final grade as receiving from the middle line identified with Jacob. Kabbalistic terminology.↩
  7. The quotation “the way of a serpent upon a rock” is Prov. 30:19; the preceding allusion to earth quaking under a servant and maidservant is Prov. 30:21–23. Oxford reads והוא הנחש, “and he is the serpent,” which is almost certainly the intended lemma: JTS line 42 immediately explains that the one who fought Jacob “is the serpent.” The present translation displays the recorded JTS reading but does not defend it. Oxford-supported correction proposed.↩
