Notes

  1. The sequence joins Prov. 30:19, Gen. 2:21, Gen. 32:27 and 32:32. The rabbinic claim that Jacob’s beauty resembled Adam’s occurs in b. Bava Batra 58a. The commentary reads Adam’s rib and Jacob’s thigh as homologous sites of damaged Judgment. Source analysis. ↩
  2. “If he merits, a helper; if not, against him” is b. Yevamot 63a. The undiminished moon invokes b. Chullin 60b. The closing phrase apparently imagines the male and female configuration before hierarchy: neither a facing counterpart nor a receiving lower grade. Rabbinic and interpretive note. ↩
  3. The terse gloss may invoke the tradition that twins were born with Cain and Abel (Genesis Rabbah 22:2). “Doubled chain” appears to describe an undiminished, paired supernal-human configuration; the exact referent remains uncertain. Provisional interpretation. ↩
  4. “You diminished him slightly” is Ps. 8:6; the displaced thigh recalls Gen. 32:26. The commentary maps the left thigh to Hod and relates its displacement to the serpent’s pollution and the withdrawal of the shining Countenance. Biblical and symbolic identifications. ↩
  5. “She pressed grapes and gave him [wine]” appears in Genesis Rabbah 19:5. “Cut the shoots” is the classic image of destructive separation in b. Chagigah 14b. Ps. 45:4 supplies “Gird your sword upon your thigh, O mighty one, your glory and majesty”; the commentary atomizes it into Gevurah and Hod. Source analysis. ↩
  6. The JTS lemma preserves Ps. 45:5, והדרך צלח, but Oxford’s והדרך נצח turns the phrase into the sefirah Netzach. The surrounding sequence strongly suggests the intended scheme: sword = Gevurah, thigh = Hod, positive commandment, Netzach, negative commandment. The folio ends mid-sentence. Text-critical note. ↩
