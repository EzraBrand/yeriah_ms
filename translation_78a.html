Notes

  1. Genesis 3:7 supplies “they sewed fig leaves,” while Proverbs 27:18 supplies “one who guards a fig tree will eat its fruit.” The commentary develops the pun תאנה / תואנה, “fig” and “pretext.” “Cutting the shoots” is the standard rabbinic image for separating the divine powers (b. Hagigah 14b). The reading “Active Intellect” remains paleographically uncertain. Biblical, rabbinic, and textual note. ↩
  2. The lion and bear appear to represent Chesed and Pachad/Gevurah. “The world follows its customary course” echoes b. Avodah Zarah 54b; the slave and maidservant come from Proverbs 30:21–23. Noah is “perfect in his generations” in Genesis 6:9. Oxford’s wording, “until there came Netzach, perfect in his generations, and Chesed above him,” may treat נצח as both a verb and a sefirotic name. Source and witness comparison. ↩
  3. Reuben’s disturbance of Jacob’s bed draws on Genesis 49:4 and the assertion in b. Shabbat 55b that Reuben did not sin. The “head of the bed” is Genesis 47:31. Joseph’s refusal of his mistress is Genesis 39; “the righteous one perishes, and no one takes it to heart” is Isaiah 57:1. The sale to the Ishmaelites comes from Genesis 37:28. “Gazed and was harmed” recalls the four who entered the orchard in b. Hagigah 14b and prepares the continuation on folio 78b. Biblical and rabbinic sources. ↩
