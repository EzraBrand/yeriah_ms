Interpretive sequence

Image Function in the commentary Sefirotic or textual reference (provisional)
The rear or heel of the Chariot A constant station there permits the primordial serpent, Esau, to gain dominion. Malkhut, following the “back” of Exodus 33:23
Jacob’s displaced thighs When the two commandments or legs lose their place, the bird of prey descends. Netzach and Hod; Genesis 32:25–33 and 15:11
The well and its stone Written Torah is apprehended through oral Torah; the cornerstone removes the serpent’s filth. Written Torah and oral Torah / Malkhut
Abram driving away the bird The covenant scene preserves the correspondence between the lower and upper Lands of Israel. Genesis 15:8–11; the exact animal-to-sefirah glosses remain uncertain

Notes

  1. Lines 1–4 complete the sentence and lemma begun at 79a:43. “The best of serpents—crush its brain” is the rabbinic maxim cited there; “the accuser becomes an advocate” echoes b. Rosh Hashanah 26a. “You shall see My back” is Exodus 33:23, read as the rear of the Chariot. The uncertain ברכת עלו may allude to Isaac’s blessing of Esau in Genesis 27:40 (ופרקת עלו). תשופך עקב reworks Genesis 3:15. Lines 5–16 identify Jacob with the middle line and the two classes of commandment with the legs; the descent of the bird of prey is Genesis 15:11. Daniel 9:19 supplies “O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive; O Lord, attend.” Several abbreviations and connective words in lines 8–15 remain unresolved. Biblical, rabbinic, and textual note. ↩
  2. The “365” are the traditional number of negative commandments. The commentary assigns “hear” to Binah, “forgive” to the middle line (Tiferet), and “attend” to Netzach. “The days” are the seven days of Creation, through which the righteous receive radiance and influx and shepherd Israel. The lemma about knowing their father’s flock adapts the patriarchal shepherd scenes, especially Genesis 37:12. Sefirotic and source note. ↩
  3. “They gathered around the well” recalls Genesis 29:3. The well is identified with the “bundle of life” of 1 Samuel 25:29 and with written Torah. “Moses undertook to explain” is Deuteronomy 1:5, followed by Deuteronomy 33:2 (“at His right hand a fiery law for them”). The stone rejected by the builders is Psalm 118:22 and is interpreted as oral Torah. “Two kings cannot use one crown” is the lunar tradition in b. Hullin 60b. The reading לשוף in lines 24–25 is uncertain and might be לשוב, influenced by Genesis 29:3; the abbreviated chain in line 29 cannot yet be expanded securely. Source and textual note. ↩
  4. “By what shall I know that I shall inherit it?” is Abraham’s question in Genesis 15:8. The phrase before ביאור in line 31, read provisionally as כתר[?] הבריכה[?], remains uncertain. The explanation that follows reads אברהם as אב רם, “exalted father,” and maps Abraham to upper Keter; “father of a multitude” also evokes Genesis 17:5. Ishmael, sent into the wilderness in Genesis 21:14, exemplifies what lies outside the Chariot. The identity of the counterpart “within” is not stated explicitly in these lines. Philological and interpretive note. ↩
  5. Lines 40–45 return to Genesis 15:9–11: the divided animals, the undivided bird, the descending birds of prey, and Abram driving them away. The commentary coordinates the lower Land of Israel with an upper Land of Israel and connects this covenant scene to Jacob’s isolation and the displaced legs earlier in the exposition. Faint interlinear words above the animals in lines 44–45 may name Pachad, Hod, Chesed, and Netzach, but they are not legible enough to attach to particular animals. The catchword אז תדע leads into 80a and Job 37:16; the two blurred marginal fragments have not been transcribed. Biblical, codicological, and textual note. ↩
