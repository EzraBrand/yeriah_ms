The chain and the commandments · Lines 1–4

The preceding warning did not concern a negative commandment; it warned through a positive commandment, as with the right thigh. When the one chain withdrew, he warned through a negative commandment, since even a slight awakening from below would remove it entirely and dislocate the thigh. The remainder is uncertain and introduces the serpent’s activity.

The hanging before the sun and Phinehas · Lines 5–14

Oxford Great Parchment:

ואמר המלאך הדובר ב[[נו]] הוקע אותם לייי׳ נגד השמש וישוב חרון אף ייי׳ מישראל ובא פנחס זה אליהו ויקח הוד וידקור את הנחש וחוה ועל כן נתן לו ברית שלום. חנוך אמר הנה מלאכי ילך לפניך.

View region

From the serpent’s powers, nourished by the left thigh: “The angel speaking in me said, ‘Hang them before the sun, and the Lord’s anger will turn back from Israel.’” The sun signifies a harsh attribute. Israel’s transgressions strengthened it and caused another power to withdraw. The angel Michael commands the hanging so that the attribute will be appeased and “iniquity shuts its mouth,” rather than destroying indiscriminately.

“Phinehas came—this is Elijah—and took a spear in his hand.” He prepared himself to awaken divine influx toward Hod, from which the serpent feeds. “He pierced the serpent and Eve”: the serpent’s pollution withdrew and the upper Eve became gentle. He therefore received the “covenant of peace,” the known prince called Enoch: “Behold, My angel will go before you.”

Egypt, creation, and אני / אין · Lines 15–23

Oxford Great Parchment:

כי במצרים השלשלת כפולה על יסו״ד מק״ו האמצעי תפאר״ת ומצות עשה תולדות השמים בה׳ בראם.

View region

A question follows: Egypt strayed after the divine name more than Israel in the time of Phinehas; why was it not judged harshly? In Egypt the chain was doubled upon the Shekhinah from the middle line, Tiferet, through Joseph’s merit.

“A positive commandment: the generations of heaven, when they were created.” Desire, signified by the positive commandment and the Shekhinah, emanated from heaven—the middle line, Tiferet. The letter he in be-hibbare’am signifies the Shekhinah. אני signifies Malkhut and is also אין, teaching that the beginning of thought is the end of action.

Elijah’s altar, Wisdom, vengeance, and the rock · Lines 24–43

“Elijah surrounded the altar with water to extinguish the calf’s fire.” The altar signifies the Crown; the water is Chesed, removing the pollution caused by Israel through the calf. Through this merit Elijah rose where no eye can rule, the hidden light. The beginning of thought becomes the end of action: “From Nothing shall my help come.”

“And Wisdom shall be found, for it was lost.” It was lost from below and the defect rose to Wisdom. “Vengeance—End.” נקם and קץ both equal 190; the attribute is the avenging sword and the End. Elijah removed the pollution in the time of wrath.

“Behold, your God will come with vengeance”; “Behold, I send you Elijah”; “you shall crush his head with the chain.” When Elijah and other exceptional righteous people unify the divine grades and attach thought to the Head, the serpent cannot harm them. Yet “you shall stand in the cleft of the rock”: the rock signifies Malkhut, necessary for the world, but thought should not remain fixed absolutely upon the material power issuing from it. Even in that temporary station, one lodges “in the shadow of Shaddai,” with the known Youth, the righteous foundation of the world. Then: “The best of serpents—crush [its head].”
